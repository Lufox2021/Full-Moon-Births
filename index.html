Full Moon Births

U.S. births per day, 1994–2014. Explore the data and test your own hypothesis.

About the data

Each row is one calendar day in the United States, with the number of babies born that day. There are 9,131 rows from two government sources:

Original files: FiveThirtyEight, github.com/fivethirtyeight/data/tree/master/births. The moon and holiday columns were added for this exercise.

Columns

ColumnMeaning
dateThe calendar day
year, month, day_of_monthParts of the date
day_of_weekMonday through Sunday
birthsNumber of births recorded for that day
sourceCDC or SSA (see left)
moon_phaseOne of eight named phases at noon that day: New moon, Waxing crescent, First quarter, Waxing gibbous, Full moon, Waning gibbous, Last quarter, Waning crescent. Each named phase covers about 3.7 days of the 29.5-day cycle.
full_moon_dateYes if the exact moment of full moon fell on this calendar day
moon_age_daysDays since the most recent new moon, at noon
moon_illuminated_pctApproximate percent of the moon's face lit, at noon
holidayU.S. federal holidays plus Christmas Eve and New Year's Eve, on their calendar dates; otherwise None

Moon times are calculated with the method in Jean Meeus, Astronomical Algorithms (2nd ed.), chapter 49, and assigned to calendar days using U.S. Eastern Standard Time.

What you can use in a formula
  • Any column name, such as births or moon_age_days
  • Arithmetic: + - * / ( ) and powers with **
  • Comparisons give 1 (true) or 0 (false): day_of_week == "Monday", births > 12000
  • Choices: condition ? a : b, for example moon_age_days < 15 ? "waxing" : "waning"
  • Functions: log(x) (natural), log10(x), sqrt(x), abs(x), round(x)
  • mean("births"): the mean of a column over all rows
  • meanBy("births", "year"): for each row, the mean of births over all rows that share its year. You can list more than one column: meanBy("births", "year", "source")
